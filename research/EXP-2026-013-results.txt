=== Reading (2026-11-10), against the pre-registered plan ===
Columns: $/yr = average dollars per year on a constant $2,500; 90% range = block-bootstrap
range for that average; worst yr = worst calendar year in dollars; up/down yrs = average
dollars in calendar years when the coin itself rose / fell; max DD = largest drop.

1. Decision rule (primary costs): the short side is NOT shown to add money (0 of 3 coins
   for every pair). At the approved cautious perps borrow (0.01%/h, about 88% of position
   size per year, charged long or short), every trend rule loses to buy-and-hold, long-only
   included. The borrow assumption dominates the result, not the direction of the trades.
2. Optimistic bound (fees only, no borrow): the short side does what the owner expected in
   bear years. In calendar years when the coin fell, long+short beat long-only on every coin
   (e.g. ETH 1-month: +$1,956/yr vs +$515/yr; BTC blend vol40: +$141/yr vs -$277/yr). It
   gave that back in up years (sharp rallies stop out shorts), so over the full period it
   helped on ETH (+$519/yr, range -$206 to +$1,294), was roughly neutral on BTC, and hurt on
   SOL. No pair's range was wholly above $0.
3. Volatility scaling roughly halved the worst drops (e.g. BTC 1-month long-only 72% -> 43%;
   ETH blend long+short 74% -> 43%) at the cost of lower average dollars.
4. Buy-and-hold made the most dollars per year on all three coins but with drops of 82-94%.
   Trend rules trade return for much smaller drops; none beat holding on dollars per year.
5. What decides this is the real cost of holding a short. The test brackets it between
   88%/yr and 0%. Next: replace the assumption with real historical funding rates (perps
   funding is paid by longs to shorts in many bull markets, which would favour shorts), then
   re-run these same eight trials unchanged.
Limits: Coinbase spot prices stand in for perps prices; 3 coins; overlapping bootstrap
blocks; calendar-year split is coarse. No parameter was tuned.
