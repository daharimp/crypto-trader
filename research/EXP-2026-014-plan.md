# EXP-2026-014 plan: the EXP-2026-013 trials with real funding rates (pre-registered)

Written and committed **before** any strategy result was computed. Every trial below is
reported, including the ones that look bad. Research only: shorts can run only in the
paper-only perps package (ADR-025, ADR-031); nothing here authorizes real-money margin or
perps (UD-24).

## Question

EXP-2026-013 found that the answer to "does the short side add money?" is decided by the
assumed cost of holding a position (0.01% per hour, about 88% of position size per year,
charged long or short). On real perpetual futures the holding cost is the **funding rate**:
when it is positive longs pay shorts, when it is negative shorts pay longs. This test replaces
the assumption with measured hourly funding and re-runs the **same eight trials, unchanged**.

## Trials (unchanged from EXP-2026-013)

T1 to T8 exactly as defined in `research/EXP-2026-013-plan.md` and imported from
`research/run_exp013.py`: TSMOM blend 1/3/12 months and TSMOM 1 month, each long+short and
long-only, each at full size and scaled to 40% volatility (never above 1x). Same 4h Coinbase
candles, same fill rule (decide at a close, fill at the next open), same 12-month warm-up,
same 60-day volatility window, same quarter-step rounding. No parameter is tuned. No leverage
above 1x. Reference: buy-and-hold of the coin (spot, no costs), as before.

## What was known when this plan was written (2026-11-12)

Only reachability and date coverage were checked. A handful of individual hourly rates were
visible in the probe output (first and last rows of each response). No average, no strategy
return, and no trial was computed.

## Data

- **Prices:** the EXP-2026-013 files (`data/long/{btc,eth,sol}_usd_4h.csv`, Coinbase spot),
  re-downloaded with `scripts/fetch_coinbase_history.py` and the same day counts.
- **Funding:** downloaded by `scripts/fetch_funding_history.py` into
  `data/funding/<source>_<coin>.csv` as one row per hour: hour start (UTC) and the rate as a
  fraction of position size for that hour, positive meaning longs pay shorts. The results file
  records the row count, first and last hour, and SHA-256 of every file used.
- **Timestamp conventions (fixed now):** a Deribit or Hyperliquid record stamped *T* covers
  the hour ending at *T*; a Kraken record stamped *T* covers the hour starting at *T*. An error
  of one hour here is immaterial for rules that hold for weeks.
- **Never invented:** an hour with no published rate stays missing. A 4h bar missing any of
  its four hours is dropped from that source's evaluation (its return and costs are not
  counted) and the number dropped is reported. If more than 1% of a window's bars would be
  dropped, that source is reported as unusable for that coin rather than patched.

## Costs

For a position of signed size *p* (fraction of the $2,500; long positive, short negative) over
one 4h bar: fee of 7 bps on every change of position (the owner-approved UD-23 value, as in
EXP-2026-013), plus *p* times the sum of the four hourly funding rates. Longs pay positive
funding and receive negative funding; shorts the reverse. Long-only trials are perps positions
too, so they pay or receive funding like any long.

- **Primary (decides the answer): Deribit funding.** Chosen before seeing results because it
  is the longest history reachable here, so it covers the most bull and bear markets.
- **Cross-checks (context only): Hyperliquid funding and Kraken Futures funding**, each on its
  own shorter window. Kraken matters most to the owner as a possible later venue, but 12
  months cannot decide anything on its own.
- **Reference rows on the primary window (context only):** fees only, and the EXP-2026-013
  assumption of 0.01% per hour, so the three cost sets can be compared over identical dates.

Evaluation window per coin and source: from the later of (end of the 12-month price warm-up,
first 4h bar fully covered by that source's funding) to the last 4h bar covered by both.

## Measures (dollars, on a constant $2,500)

As in EXP-2026-013: average dollars per year with a 90% range by circular block bootstrap
(30-day blocks, 2,000 resamples, fixed seed), worst calendar year, average over the coin's up
years and down years, largest peak-to-trough drop, and the paired difference "long+short minus
long-only" with its 90% range. Added, descriptive only: each source's average funding per
year as a percent of position size, the share of hours with positive funding, and the average
funding dollars paid or received per year by each trial.

## Decision rule (fixed in advance)

At **primary** costs, a long+short rule **passes** only if both hold on **at least 2 of 3
coins**:

1. the 90% range of (long+short minus the same rule long-only) is above $0 per year, and
2. the 90% range of the long+short rule's own result is above $0 per year.

Condition 1 is the EXP-2026-013 rule. Condition 2 is added so that a rule cannot pass merely
by losing less than its long-only twin.

- **If a rule passes:** it becomes a named signal for the forward paper perps session
  (`trader perps paper run --signal <name>`), never real money. If more than one passes, the
  one with the highest sum over the three coins of the lower end of its own 90% range is
  implemented. The session's signal only says long, short, or flat; sizing stays with the
  deterministic risk code, so for a volatility-scaled trial only the direction is implemented
  and the scaling is put to the owner as a separate decision.
- **If none passes:** the answer is "not shown", stated plainly, and the next pre-registered
  test is proposed with its published sources. No parameter is changed after seeing results.
- A pass on Deribit that has the opposite sign on Hyperliquid or Kraken over their windows is
  reported as fragile next to the pass. Cross-checks cannot turn a fail into a pass.

## Known limits (stated before running)

- Four pairs on three coins are tested, so one chance pass is possible; a single marginal pass
  is to be read with that in mind.
- Coinbase spot prices stand in for each venue's perps price; the gap between them (the
  basis) is not modelled.
- Funding differs between venues. Deribit does not serve US customers, so its rates stand in
  for what a US venue would have charged; the Kraken cross-check covers one year only.
- The Deribit window starts in 2019 for BTC and ETH and in 2022 for SOL, so it is shorter than
  the EXP-2026-013 window and the two experiments' dollar figures are not directly comparable;
  the reference rows give the like-for-like comparison.
- Past funding does not predict future funding. No result here is a forecast or a promise of
  profit.
