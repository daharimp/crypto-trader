=== Reading (2026-11-12), against the pre-registered plan ===
Columns as in EXP-2026-013, plus "funding $/yr": funding dollars per year on $2,500
(negative = paid, positive = received). Deribit decides; Hyperliquid and Kraken are context.

1. Decision rule: NO rule passes. On Deribit funding the 90% range of (long+short minus
   long-only) includes $0 for every pair on every coin (0 of 3), so the short side is still
   "not shown" to add money. Nothing is added to the paper session from this experiment.
2. The holding-cost assumption was the problem in EXP-2026-013. Measured funding averaged
   about +8% of position size per year on BTC and +6% on ETH (Deribit, 2019-2026), about 0%
   on SOL (Deribit, 2022-2026), and +12% to +14% on Hyperliquid (2023-2026), against the 88%
   assumed. In dollars the trend rules paid roughly $90 to $370 a year in funding on $2,500,
   not the roughly $1,100+ a year the assumption charged. Over identical dates, the "real
   funding" table sits close to the "fees only" table and far from the "0.01%/h" table.
3. With real funding the long+short rules made money on their own on BTC and ETH: all four
   have a 90% range above $0 there (e.g. BTC 1-month vol-scaled T7: +$1,034/yr, range $349 to
   $1,721; ETH T7: +$1,276/yr, range $667 to $1,927). On SOL every range includes $0. That is
   condition 2 of the rule (2 of 3 coins) met; condition 1 (beating long-only) is what failed.
4. What the short side did: in years when the coin fell, long+short made money where
   long-only lost or made less (BTC T7 +$238 vs T8 -$184; ETH T5 +$1,891 vs T6 +$575), and
   it gave part of that back in rising years. Net over the whole period: about +$70 to +$270
   a year for the 1-month rule on BTC and ETH, negative for the 1/3/12 blend and for SOL, and
   every one of those ranges spans $0. Adding shorts also deepened the worst drop (BTC T8 33%
   -> T7 52%).
5. Shorts do not collect funding for free. Funding was positive in most hours, but trend
   rules are short mainly in falling markets, when funding turns low or negative, and long in
   rising markets, when it is high. So the long+short rules paid about as much funding as the
   long-only ones (BTC, ETH) or twice as much (SOL), instead of earning it back.
6. Buy-and-hold still made the most dollars on BTC ($1,340/yr) and SOL; on ETH the 1-month
   long+short rule's average was higher ($2,001 vs $1,603) with overlapping ranges, so that
   is not a finding. The trend rules' worst drops were 31-68% on BTC/ETH against 77-81% for
   holding.
7. Cross-checks. Hyperliquid (3.4 years): same picture, short side positive on ETH, negative
   on BTC and SOL, all ranges span $0. Kraken (12 months, a falling year for all three
   coins): long+short ahead of long-only by $430 to $1,060 a year on every pair, but every
   range is about $2,500 to $4,000 wide and spans $0. One falling year cannot show anything.

What this does and does not apply to: venues that charge funding between longs and shorts
(Kraken Futures, Hyperliquid, Deribit, and Jupiter's beta markets). Jupiter's SOL/ETH/BTC
markets charge an hourly borrow fee to both sides instead; no historical series of that fee
was found, so those markets remain untested with real data. The paper session's 0.01%/h is
the owner's approved cautious value and is not changed here.

Limits (as pre-registered): four pairs on three coins; Coinbase spot stands in for perps
prices; Deribit does not serve US customers; SOL funding on Deribit was thinly traded early
on; windows are shorter than EXP-2026-013. One Deribit bar per coin and up to six Kraken bars
were dropped for a missing hour, none filled in. No parameter was tuned. Past results do not
predict future results.

=== Proposed next pre-registered test (EXP-2026-015; plan to be committed before any run) ===
The eight trials have now been seen on BTC, ETH and SOL, so any new rule tried on those three
coins and dates is no longer a clean test. Each option below therefore uses coins or data not
yet looked at, and ends in forward paper trading.
A. (recommended) Shorts only in a confirmed downtrend: the same 1-month TSMOM, but a short is
   allowed only while price is below its 200-day average (Faber 2007, "A Quantitative
   Approach to Tactical Asset Allocation", J. Wealth Management). Motivated by reading 4
   (shorts lose in rising years), so it must be judged on coins not used here (for example
   XRP, DOGE, LTC, LINK, AVAX, ADA with Coinbase prices and Hyperliquid funding), with the
   same decision rule.
B. Cross-sectional momentum: each week hold the recent winners long and the recent losers
   short across about ten coins (Liu, Tsyvinski & Wu 2022, "Common Risk Factors in
   Cryptocurrency", J. Finance). Roughly market-neutral, so funding paid and received mostly
   cancel. Needs funding for ten coins (Hyperliquid, 2023 onward: a short history).
C. Volatility-managed size on the long+short rule (Moreira & Muir 2017, "Volatility-Managed
   Portfolios", J. Finance; Barroso & Santa-Clara 2015, "Momentum Has Its Moments", JFE).
   Largely covered already by the vol-scaled trials (T3, T4, T7, T8), which cut the worst
   drops but did not change the long+short versus long-only answer; lowest priority.
