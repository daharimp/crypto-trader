# EXP-2026-015 plan: how should opening leverage be chosen? (pre-registered)

Written and committed **before** any result was computed. Every policy below is reported,
including the ones that look bad. Research only: nothing here changes a config value or the
paper session; the owner decides what to do with the findings. Paper perps only (ADR-025,
ADR-031); real-money margin or perps is not authorized by anything here (UD-24).

## Questions (owner, 2026-11-12, relayed by the main session; UD-26, UD-27, ADR-032)

The paper session now assesses each opening leverage from the market's 30-day typical daily
move (ADR-032), up to a 25x ceiling. The owner asked:

1. Does volatility-assessed leverage beat a fixed 12x?
2. Should opening leverage ALSO be raised when the signal is strong (a "good position"), not
   only when the market is calm?

## Position model (mirrors the paper session; fixed now)

One market at a time, on a **constant $2,500**: every trade posts $250 collateral (10% of
$2,500, UD-23) and never compounds. Size is collateral times the opening leverage, fixed for
the life of the trade. Fees are 7 bps of size on open and on close; maintenance is 20 bps of
size (approved venue values). A decision made at a bar's close fills at the next bar's open.
Each bar, funding is accrued, then the position is checked against the bar's adverse extreme
(low for a long, high for a short): if the collateral left after the closing fee and accrued
funding is at or below maintenance, the trade is **liquidated and the whole $250 is lost**
(the simulator's conservative rule). After a liquidation the signal may open a new trade at
the next bar. Leverage is always between 1x and 25x (the approved maximum).

Not modelled: several markets sharing one account (the 400% total-size cap, the 3-position
limit, the 15% daily-loss halt), compounding, and running out of money; cumulative losses are
simply reported in dollars.

## Direction signals (only say long, short, or flat)

- **Primary:** two-sided 1-month time-series momentum (long if the trailing 30-day return is
  positive, short if negative; Moskowitz, Ooi & Pedersen 2012). This is the direction rule
  that made money on its own on BTC and ETH in EXP-2026-014, so these coins and dates are no
  longer unseen for it; this test is about the leverage policy, not about the signal.
- **Context only:** `sma_stance` 20/50, the signal the paper session runs today.

## Leverage policies

| ID | Policy | Opening leverage |
|---|---|---|
| F3, F6, F12, F25 | fixed | 3x, 6x, 12x, 25x on every trade (F12 is the comparator, UD-25) |
| V | assessed by volatility (ADR-032 as approved) | `1 / (4 x daily move + closing fee + maintenance)`, rounded down to 0.1, between 1x and 25x |
| VS1-raise | volatility + trend strength, raise only | as V, but 3 daily moves instead of 4 when the signal is strong |
| VS1-tilt | volatility + trend strength, raise and lower | 3 daily moves when strong, 5 when weak, 4 otherwise |
| VS2-raise | volatility + agreement, raise only | as V, but 3 daily moves when the signal is strong |
| VS2-tilt | volatility + agreement, raise and lower | 3 daily moves when strong, 5 when weak, 4 otherwise |

The daily move is ADR-032's: the standard deviation of 4h returns over the last 30 days,
scaled to one day, from closes up to the decision bar. The buffer steps (3 and 5 around the
owner's 4) are experiment values chosen before running and are not tuned afterwards.

**Signal strength (two published definitions, fixed now):**

- **S1, trend strength:** the size of the trailing 30-day return divided by what a typical
  30-day move would be (daily move times the square root of 30). Strong: at least 1. Weak:
  below 0.5. This is the return-over-volatility trend measure used in Baltas & Kosowski
  (2013, "Momentum Strategies in Futures Markets and Trend-Following Funds") and Lempérière et
  al. (2014, "Two Centuries of Trend Following").
- **S2, agreement across lookbacks:** the 1-, 3- and 12-month trailing returns (Hurst, Ooi &
  Pedersen 2017). Strong: both the 3- and 12-month returns point the same way as the trade.
  Weak: both point against it. Otherwise normal.

The "raise only" versions are the owner's literal question. They also use more leverage on
average, so the "tilt" versions (raise when strong, lower when weak) are included to separate
"strength helps" from "more leverage helps".

## Data and costs

- Prices: the EXP-2026-014 Coinbase 4h files (BTC, ETH, SOL), including highs and lows for the
  liquidation check. Evaluation windows: the EXP-2026-014 Deribit windows (BTC and ETH from
  2019-04-30, SOL from 2022-06-15), each starting flat.
- **Primary holding cost (decides): Deribit hourly funding**, as in EXP-2026-014 (ADR-033):
  longs pay positive funding, shorts receive it. EXP-2026-014 found one 4h bar per coin with
  a missing hour; because a position's path cannot skip a bar, that bar is charged the hours
  that exist and the count is reported.
- **Context only:** the paper session's approved 0.01% per hour borrow fee charged on size to
  both sides (UD-23), because that is what the paper session will actually show.

## Measures (dollars, on a constant $2,500)

Per policy and coin: average dollars per year with a 90% range (circular block bootstrap,
30-day blocks, 2,000 resamples, fixed seed), worst calendar year, largest peak-to-trough drop
in dollars, number of trades, number of liquidations and dollars lost to them per year,
average opening leverage, and funding dollars per year. Paired differences with 90% ranges:
V minus F12, and each VS policy minus V.

Diagnostic that does not depend on any leverage mapping: under policy V, the average result
per $100 of position for trades opened on a strong signal against all other trades, for S1
and for S2, with a 90% range for the difference (trades resampled 2,000 times).

## Decision rules (fixed in advance, primary signal and primary cost)

- **Q1:** volatility-assessed leverage "beats fixed 12x" only if the 90% range of (V minus
  F12) is above $0 per year on **at least 2 of 3 coins**. If the range is below $0 on at
  least 2 of 3, fixed 12x "beats" it. Otherwise: not shown either way.
- **Q2:** raising leverage on signal strength "adds money" only if, for the same VS policy,
  the 90% range of (VS minus V) is above $0 per year on **at least 2 of 3 coins**. A
  raise-only pass whose tilt twin does not pass is reported as "more leverage, not strength".
- Whether any policy is worth running at all is reported the same way: its own 90% range
  above $0 on at least 2 of 3 coins.

## Known limits (stated before running)

- The primary signal was selected after seeing these coins and dates (EXP-2026-014), so
  dollar levels are optimistic; differences between leverage policies are less affected but
  not immune. The forward paper session is the clean test.
- Four VS policies are compared with V on three coins, so one chance pass is possible.
- Coinbase spot highs and lows stand in for the perps mark price; real perps wicks can be
  deeper or shallower, which matters most at high leverage. Liquidation is checked once per
  4h bar and loses the whole collateral.
- Deribit funding stands in for the venue; Jupiter's SOL/ETH/BTC markets charge a borrow fee
  instead (context rows).
- One market at a time on constant capital: account-level limits and compounding are not
  modelled, so these are not forecasts of the paper session's account. No result here is a
  forecast or a promise of profit.
