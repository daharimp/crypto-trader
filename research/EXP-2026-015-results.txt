=== Reading (2026-11-12), against the pre-registered plan ===
Columns: $/yr = average dollars per year on a constant $2,500 ($250 collateral per trade, one
coin at a time); 90% range = block-bootstrap range for that average; worst yr = worst calendar
year; max drop = largest peak-to-trough fall in dollars; liqs = liquidations; liq $/yr =
dollars lost to liquidations per year; avg lev = average opening leverage; funding $/yr =
funding or borrow paid (negative) per year. F = fixed leverage, V = assessed from volatility
(ADR-032), VS = volatility plus signal strength.

1. Q1, does volatility-assessed leverage beat fixed 12x? NOT SHOWN EITHER WAY. V made less
   than fixed 12x on all three coins (BTC -$160/yr, ETH -$1,073, SOL -$203); only the ETH
   range is wholly below $0. The reason is simple: V opened at lower leverage on average
   (9.7x BTC, 8.2x ETH, 6.4x SOL), and less leverage means fewer dollars both ways.
2. What V bought was smaller drops and fewer liquidations: worst drop $3,020 vs $3,179 (BTC),
   $1,818 vs $4,146 (ETH), $4,584 vs $6,877 (SOL); liquidations 5 vs 14, 2 vs 17, 4 vs 35.
   Dollars earned per dollar of worst drop: BTC 0.64 vs 0.66 (the same), ETH 0.89 vs 0.65,
   SOL 0.32 vs 0.25. That comparison is descriptive, not a pre-registered test.
3. Q2, should leverage also rise when the signal is strong? NOT SHOWN for any of the four
   versions (0 of 3 coins each).
   - Agreement across lookbacks (VS2): +$264, +$153, +$16 a year over V, every range spans
     $0, and it used about 10% more leverage. Trade by trade, strong-agreement trades were not
     shown to be better per $100 of position (BTC +1.02, range -0.95 to +3.51; ETH -0.24;
     SOL -2.06, both ranges spanning $0).
   - Trend strength (VS1) could not be tested with this signal. A 1-month trend trade opens
     at the moment the 30-day return crosses zero, so the "strength" at the open is always
     near zero: 1, 0 and 2 strong opens out of 500, 504 and 281. VS1-raise is therefore V,
     and VS1-tilt is just V with a wider buffer (lower leverage, fewer dollars; its range is
     below $0 on BTC and ETH). This is a design limit found in the run, not evidence against
     the idea; the plan's definitions were not changed.
4. Leverage is a volume knob, not an edge. With fixed leverage, dollars per year and the
   worst drop both scale almost exactly with the leverage (BTC: 3x $525 and $795; 6x $1,054
   and $1,589; 12x $2,089 and $3,179; 25x $4,405 and $6,622). On a $2,500 account, fixed 12x
   had a worst drop larger than the whole account on every coin, and V on BTC and SOL. This
   replay keeps betting $250 after losses, so it does not show an account running out; a real
   account would. At 25x, liquidations alone cost $2,936 to $8,016 a year.
5. Holding cost decides whether any of this is positive. With Deribit funding every policy
   made money on BTC and ETH (own 90% range above $0; SOL ranges span $0). With the paper
   session's approved 0.01%/h borrow, no policy is shown to make money on any coin: at V's
   leverage the borrow fee is about $1,400 to $2,200 a year on $2,500, and $2,628 at fixed
   12x, which is larger than the trend profits. The paper session uses that borrow value, so
   it should be expected to show losses at high leverage even with a better signal.
6. Today's paper signal (`sma_stance` 20/50, context only): not shown to make money on BTC or
   ETH at any leverage (every range spans $0; SOL under V is marginal, $6 to $2,539). It was
   liquidated far more often (fixed 12x: 49, 113, 69 times; V: 20, 15, 8). Here V beat fixed
   12x on BTC (+$485/yr, range $33 to $969) because it avoided liquidations; ETH and SOL
   ranges span $0.

What the owner can take from this:
- Volatility-assessed leverage (UD-26) is a reasonable risk control: fewer liquidations and
  smaller drops for somewhat fewer dollars. It is not shown to make more money than fixed 12x.
- Nothing here supports raising leverage further on signal strength.
- The ceiling of 25x (UD-27) is reached only in calm markets under V; a fixed 25x would have
  been liquidated 87 to 139 times per coin.
- The two things that mattered most are the direction signal and the holding cost, not the
  leverage rule.

Limits (as pre-registered): the 1-month trend rule was chosen after seeing these coins and
dates, so its dollar levels are optimistic; Coinbase spot highs and lows stand in for perps
wicks; Deribit funding stands in for the venue; one coin at a time on constant capital, so
account-level limits, compounding, and running out of money are not modelled; four VS
versions on three coins. One Deribit bar on BTC and one on ETH had a missing funding hour and
were charged the hours that exist. The engine's dollar total was cross-checked against an
independent calculation (BTC, fixed 3x: $3,956.99 both ways, 498 trades). No parameter was
tuned. Past results do not predict future results.
