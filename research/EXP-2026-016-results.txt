=== Reading (2026-11-12), against the pre-registered plan ===
Columns: $/yr = average dollars per year on a constant $2,500 ($250 collateral per trade, one
coin at a time, assessed opening leverage); 90% range = block-bootstrap range; max drop =
largest peak-to-trough fall in dollars; $/yr per $ drop = the two divided (descriptive);
in market = share of bars with a side signalled; funding $/yr = funding or borrow paid.

1. VERDICT BY THE PRE-REGISTERED RULE: CONTRADICTED. The clause that triggered it is the
   Panel B one: on the five coins never looked at before, the consensus made fewer average
   dollars than SMA alone on 4 of 5 (DOGE -$202, LTC -$126, LINK -$295, ADA -$103 a year;
   AVAX +$27). Every one of those differences has a 90% range that spans $0, so this is a
   consistent direction, not a measured gap. The clause was written on averages before the
   run and is reported as written.
2. Panel A (BTC, ETH, SOL; not unseen data): the consensus beat SMA alone on BTC (+$465/yr,
   range $75 to $839) and ETH (+$511, $137 to $868). Against TSMOM alone it made less on all
   three (BTC -$812, range -$1,516 to -$154; ETH -$538, range -$1,110 to +$36; SOL -$434,
   range spans $0): "not shown either way" under the 2-of-3 rule. Its own range was above $0
   on all three coins.
3. Panel B (unseen, about 3.4 years): the consensus's own range was above $0 on 1 of 5 coins
   (AVAX), and it was never at least as good as both single rules. TSMOM alone, the best rule
   on Panel A, was not the best here: it led on DOGE and AVAX only, with the largest drops
   everywhere ($2,301 to $4,731). SMA alone, the weakest rule on BTC and ETH, had the highest
   average on LINK and ADA. LTC lost money under every signal. So neither single rule is
   shown to be the better one across both panels either.
4. What the consensus does, consistently: it is flat about a third of the time (in market 60%
   to 66%), so it earns less than the better single rule on each coin and falls less. Its
   largest drop was the smallest of the three on 6 of 8 coins (all but DOGE and ADA), and it
   had the fewest or equal-fewest liquidations on 7 of 8. Dollars per dollar of largest drop:
   best or tied on BTC, SOL and AVAX, in the middle elsewhere. These are descriptive.
5. Holding cost: at the paper session's 0.0016%/h borrow (UD-28) the picture is the same as
   with measured funding; the borrow fee is about $140 to $350 a year at these leverages.

What the owner can take from this:
- The evidence does not support the consensus as the best default on dollars. It also does
  not show either single rule to be better across seen and unseen coins.
- The consensus is the lower-drop, fewer-liquidations choice and gives up dollars for it.
  Whether that trade is wanted is a risk preference, not something this test can decide.
- The single rules disagree about which is best depending on the coins and years, which is a
  warning against picking a default from one history. The forward paper session is the test.
