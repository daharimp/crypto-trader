Why the ladders lose (perps-main, BTC): the top 10% of trades earn more than all trades
combined (174% of net R); 72% of gross profit comes from trades above +4R; the best trade
was +54R. A TP3 cap stops every trade at +4.5R or less, and stops that trail or ratchet exit
the big trends in their normal pullbacks. Waiting (or not) for the next UTC day before
re-entering does not change this (checked).

Conclusion: a fixed hard stop placed at entry keeps the trend strategies' return and caps
the worst trade near -1R (vs -2.7 to -5R with no stop). Every rule that moves the stop up or
sells early (breakeven, chandelier, ratchets, the TP ladder) gave up more than it protected,
in all three bots and in both periods, and none lowered max drawdown reliably. Consistent
with Kaminski & Lo (2014) and Clare et al. (2013): stops help trend strategies by cutting
losers, while trend profits come from a few uncapped winners.
Caveats: spot candles stand in for perps prices; funding not modeled; equal size for all
methods (a tighter stop could justify larger size); results are hypotheses, not promises.
