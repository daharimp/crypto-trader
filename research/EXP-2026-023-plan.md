# EXP-2026-023 plan: why the paper perps bots lose, and what to change first

Written 2026-11-26. Research only; changes no configuration.

Honesty note on timing: the replay harness and the first variant table for `main` and
`spot-perps` (section "Base table") were run before this file was written; the variant
list was fixed in `research/run_exp023.py` before that run and has not been edited since.
The four follow-up studies below had their grids written into their scripts before any
holdout number for them was seen.

## Question
Owner (2026-11-26): "I don't like this trading bot's performance, how can I improve it now.
Was there no logs or data we can look at and improve?"

## What the live data can support
The current runs started 2026-11-22 22:12-22:53 UTC. By 2026-11-24 00:20 UTC the three perps
accounts had closed nine trades: the same three shorts (BTC, ETH, SOL) on each account, all
stopped out on 2026-11-23 by one two-day bounce. Nine trades that are one decision cannot
separate a bad rule from a normal losing week, so the diagnosis rests on a replay.

## Method
`research/exp023_lib.py` drives the real paper account (`PaperPerpsAccount`), the live
config files and the live signal `trend_50_200_range_10_30` over Binance candles for BTC,
ETH and SOL (2020-01, SOL 2020-08, to 2026-11-21), all three coins in one compounding
account, in the same order of steps as `trader.perps.session.cycle`. Earlier experiments
(EXP-010 to EXP-022) replayed one coin at a time on constant capital; this is the first
replay of the accounts as they actually run.

- Development: 2020-10-01 to 2023-12-31. Holdout: 2024-01-01 to 2026-11-21.
- Each run starts fresh at $2,500.
- Costs from the config: 7 bps a side, borrow 0.0016% an hour. Funding is not modelled.
- Reported: return per year, Sharpe (daily), max drawdown, entries per month, share of
  entries ending at the stop, fees per year as % of average equity, worst day.

## Base table (`research/run_exp023.py`)
One change at a time against each account's live setting: the 50/200 rule without the
range switch; a cap on same-side open risk (3%, 4.5%, 6% of equity); liquidation buffer 6,
8, 10, 12 daily moves (lower leverage and a wider price stop at the same 30% of
collateral); breakout re-entry after a stop; "no ladder" as a reference only; and for
spot-perps the ADR-045 ladder, a 10% stop and 25% collateral.

## Follow-up studies (run in parallel)
1. `exp023_signal.py`: where the profit comes from (exit reason, regime, side, coin), a
   fixed grid of churn-reducing signal variants, and cost sensitivity at 0x to 3x.
2. `exp023_sizing.py`: collateral x buffer x group-cap frontier against the go-live bar
   (max drawdown under 15%), whether a group cap beats simply posting less collateral, and
   robustness by calendar year, rolling 12-month windows and first-100-trades samples.
3. `exp023_degen.py`: the hourly account, whether hourly execution adds anything over 4h,
   and how different the three accounts really are.
4. An independent audit of the harness against the live engine (look-ahead, fill timing,
   accounting).

## Selection rule
Rank on development only. A change is recommended only if it also does not lose to the
live setting in the holdout on Sharpe and max drawdown, and if neighbouring settings agree
(no isolated peaks). A holdout Sharpe difference of about 0.1 on three years is treated as
noise. Changes to risk values (leverage, stop size, position size) are presented to the
owner with the evidence and are not merged without a yes.

## Known limits
- Binance spot candles, while the paper bots read Kraken prices and model Jupiter costs.
- Stops and take-profits fill at their level unless the bar opens through them; real fills
  in fast markets are worse.
- A backtest drawdown is a floor for what live trading will show, not a forecast.
