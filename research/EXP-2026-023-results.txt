EXP-2026-023: why the paper perps accounts perform as they do, and what a replay supports
=========================================================================================
Date: 2026-11-26. Research only; changes no configuration. Plan: EXP-2026-023-plan.md.
Simulated results on historical data with modelled costs. Not a forecast; profit is not
guaranteed or implied.

Method
- research/exp023_lib.py drives the real paper account (trader.perps.simulator), the live
  configs and the live signal over stored candles, BTC, ETH and SOL in one compounding
  account, in the same order of steps as trader.perps.session.cycle.
- Windows: development 2020-10-01..2023-12-31, validation 2024-01-01..2026-11-21. The
  validation window has been read by about 22 experiments, so it is a check, not a test.
- Independent audit: EXP-2026-023-audit.md (matches the live session event for event, no
  look-ahead; known issues listed there; the tables below came from a verified cache).
- Columns: CAGR, Sharpe (daily, 365 days), max drawdown, entries a month, share of entries
  closed by a stop (including stops a take-profit had moved), fees + borrow a year as % of
  average equity, worst day. Left block development, right block validation.

Findings
1. Nothing in the live journal is a malfunction. Nine closed trades are one bet: the same
   three shorts on three accounts, stopped on 2026-11-23. A replay from 2026-11-22
   reproduces direction, leverage (6.9/7.1/9.2x against 7.0/7.1/9.2x), stop day and
   re-entry.
2. The three leveraged accounts are one test at three sizes: daily-return correlation
   0.89-0.93 in 2024-2026 (0.74-0.91 before), never on opposite sides at a 4h close.
   BTC/ETH/SOL daily correlation 0.78-0.82 in 2024-2026; all three signals are on the same
   side on 68% of 4h bars.
3. Main as configured: a day of -7% or worse 12.3 (development) and 19.0 (validation)
   times a year; a 9% loss inside 7 days in 16.7% / 19.6% of weeks; max drawdown 57% / 74%.
   The go-live bar (drawdown under 15%) fails in 62 of 62 rolling 12-month windows.
4. Position size is a dial: return and drawdown scale together and Sharpe does not move
   (EXP-2026-023-sizing-results.txt). Under 15% in both windows needs about 2% collateral.
5. The opening stop is the largest cost (-254 / -235 % of equity a year), four to five
   times the fee bill (55 / 69 %, about half of it borrow). Profit comes from trades held
   more than seven days (EXP-2026-023-signal-results.txt).
6. A wider volatility stop (liquidation buffer 8 instead of 4, about half the leverage)
   cuts stop-outs from 26-29% of entries to 6-7% at a similar Sharpe. EXP-2026-024 then
   found it is no better than half size on unseen 2018-2019 data.
7. No signal variant beats the live one outside noise: plain 50/200, an EMA vote of three
   speeds, breakout re-entry, hysteresis, flat in ranges. The development winner was the
   worst in validation.
8. A same-side open-risk cap is not worth building: as a first-come cap it funds the first
   coin to flip, the weakest entry (the entries it cuts down earn about twice as much per
   dollar as the ones it leaves whole), and it has a higher Sharpe than the uncapped setting
   of the same exposure in only 3 of 20 pairs in each window. An equal split is just
   smaller collateral.
9. Degen: deciding every hour from the unfinished 4h candle costs 0.26-0.43 Sharpe at
   identical settings (EXP-2026-023-degen-results.txt).
10. A take-profit ladder on spot-perps lowers development return from 82.7% to 37.0% and
    is neutral in validation.
11. Costs decide everything: on validation, growth turns negative at about 1.8x the
    modelled costs at buffer 4 and about 2.1x at buffer 8 (development: 2.7x and 4.0x).
