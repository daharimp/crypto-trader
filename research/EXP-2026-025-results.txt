EXP-2026-025: three add-ons for the top-ten hourly account
================================================================================
Date: 2026-11-26. Research only; changes no configuration. Plan (committed before any
add-on was run): EXP-2026-025-plan.md. Run: research/run_exp025.py; harness:
research/exp025_lib.py. Simulated results on historical data with modelled costs. Not a
forecast; profit is not guaranteed or implied.

B0 is the account the owner chose (ten coins, hourly decisions, 1% of equity posted per
position, buffer 8, 5x ceiling, 30% stop, 4/8/12R ladder), at 100 times its $2,500 balance.
Engine: the one merged with PR 69 (borrow accrues exactly).

Columns: CAGR, Sharpe (daily, 365 days), max drawdown, worst day, entries a month,
decisions a month (coins opened on the same UTC day on the same side count once), share of
entries closed by the opening stop, fees + borrow a year as % of average equity, how often
the add-on acted a month (a waiting window that refused an open, a time-limit exit, or a
position sized below full); the Sharpe difference from B0 with a 90% interval (paired
block bootstrap of daily returns, 10-day blocks, 2,000 draws); then CAGR, Sharpe and max
drawdown at twice the fees and twice the borrow rate.

How the rule was applied (fixed in run_exp025.py before any add-on row was read): a
setting's neighbours are the settings next to it in its own grid (an edge setting has one,
the central setting has two; the two correlation modes are each other's). "Agree in sign"
on a slice means every neighbour moved Sharpe the same way as the setting did.

Verdicts under the plan's rule
- Cooldown after a losing trade (CD): HELPS at 4, 12 and 24 hours. Sharpe is higher than
  B0's on 4, 3 and 4 of the four slices; the one lower cell is 12 hours on 2018-2019
  (-0.04, inside the 0.05 the rule allows). The gain is largest at 24 hours on three
  slices and about zero on 2018-2019. On the 2024-2026 slice and on the ten other majors
  the interval excludes zero at every length.
  FLAG: it removes more than a quarter of the entries at every length: 26% at 4 hours, 38%
  at 12, 46% at 24. Separate decisions fall less: 8%, 13% and 19%. The owner asked for
  more trades, so the length is the owner's call.
  At twice the costs it adds 2 to 9 points a year on every slice (CAGR, B0 / 4h / 12h /
  24h: S1 5.3 / 8.3 / 9.0 / 10.6; S2 -2.0 / 2.2 / 3.9 / 6.6; S3 -6.8 / -3.4 / -1.6 / 0.0;
  S4 3.8 / 6.3 / 5.7 / 7.2). Fees and borrow fall from 9-11% of equity a year to 8-9% at
  4 hours, 7-8% at 12 and 6-8% at 24.
- Stop guard (SG): NO BENEFIT. It acts once every one to two months. The central
  setting (2 stops in 7 days, pause 7 days) is lower than B0 on all four slices (-0.00 to
  -0.07; the rule's label is "hurts" because of -0.07 on 2018-2019); the two neighbours
  are within 0.05 everywhere ("does no harm"). Not recommended: the account already makes
  a stopped coin wait until the next UTC day, and an opening stop ends only 3% to 4% of
  its entries.
- Correlation sizing (VC): DOES NOT PASS. Volatility sizing is already how the account
  works (every position risks the same 0.3% of equity at its stop).
  VC-tiers has a higher Sharpe than B0 on all four slices (+0.00 to +0.06), but every 90%
  interval includes zero and its neighbour VC-root agrees on only two slices, so it is not
  "helps". The rule then labels it "hurts" only because its gain on 2018-2019 (+0.057) is
  outside the 0.05 band that "does no harm" was written with; that band should have been
  one-sided. Read plainly: no reliable effect on Sharpe. Against a flat account of the same
  average size (0.95% to 0.96%) it lowers the worst day on every slice (-3.2 vs -3.8,
  -2.1 vs -2.2, -4.5 vs -5.0, -4.0 vs -4.1) and the largest drop on two (14 vs 15, 21 vs
  23). It never changes the number of trades. Optional: it goes in only if the owner still
  wants it.
  VC-root (the stronger form) is lower on two slices, by 0.27 on the ten other majors with
  an interval that excludes zero: it hurts. Not recommended.
- Time limit (TL): HURTS at 24, 72 and 168 hours on three of four slices (Sharpe -0.83 to
  -0.07; at 24 hours the interval excludes zero on all three). It helps only on 2018-2019.
  This agrees with EXP-2026-023: the profit of a trend rule comes from trades held longer
  than a week, and many of those are under water after a day or three. Recommended
  against.
- Combination: only the cooldown helps, so the combination the plan asks for is the
  cooldown alone; there is nothing further to run.

What the cooldown changes (exploratory: measured after the tables were read; the table
is at the end). In B0 about 63% of entries open within 4 hours of a losing exit on the
same coin, and the median trade lasts 8 hours: the hourly account re-reads the unfinished
4h candle every hour, so a signal can flip out and back within hours. These re-entries are
not losers on average (mean result 0.02R to 0.04R, against -0.01R to 0.03R for the other
entries), but only 29% to 31% of them win, against 40% to 45% of the others. They are
chains of quick in-and-out trades that end with the one entry that catches the move. A
cooldown waits out the chain and enters once, later. So "the entries it removes lose
money" would be the wrong reading; what it saves is the chain's fees and small losses,
and what it risks is a later entry into a real move. The tables show the net: higher
Sharpe on three slices and no change on 2018-2019. This is the same effect EXP-2026-024
measured from the other side (hourly decisions add 60% more entries and lower the return
on every slice): a longer cooldown moves the hourly account back toward the 4h one.
For the purpose of gathering data, most of the entries a cooldown removes repeat a bet
the account had just made on that coin; the count of separate decisions falls far less.
