# EXP-2026-026 plan: does any confidence measure earn a size range?

Written 2026-11-26 02:35 UTC and committed before any measure was computed. Research only;
changes no configuration. Simulated results on historical data are not a forecast; profit
is not guaranteed or implied.

## Why
Owner, 2026-11-26: "it was always a range so not every trade needed to be 10% a position it
should be assessed based on confidence", and risk must be a setting each user chooses. Today
every trade posts the same share of the account (`max_collateral_per_position_pct`); only
the leverage varies, with volatility. The owner's first range (5 to 15% by confidence,
2026-11-15) was replayed twice and its score did not predict winners (highest value 66%;
trades rated 60 to 70% made money 41% of the time), so it was never built. This plan tests
better measures before any of them is trusted with size. The range itself (smallest and
largest size per trade) is the owner's to set; this experiment only decides whether a
measure may choose inside it.

## Accounts and slices
A: `main` as configured except for size (SOL, ETH, BTC; 4h decisions; buffer 4; 30% stop;
ladder), on
- A1 2020-10-01 to 2023-12-31, A2 2024-01-01 to 2026-11-24 (both used before; the breadth
  hint below came from them);
- A3 BTC, ETH, LTC, 2018-02-01 to 2019-12-31 (not used for any sizing question).
B: the top-ten hourly account (B0 of EXP-2026-025: 1% collateral, buffer 8, 5x ceiling) on
the four slices of EXP-2026-025: B1 `dev10`, B2 `hold10`, B3 ten other majors of 2021,
B4 ten majors of 2018 (2018-08 to 2019-12).
Seven slices. A3, B3 and B4 share no period or no coin with where the hint was found, and
are called the fresh slices. Balance 100 times $2,500, as in EXP-2026-024.

## Measures (each a score from 0 to 1, from completed candles up to the decision only)
- **M1 breadth.** Share of the account's other coins whose signal at this decision is on
  the same side as the new position.
- **M2 trend strength.** The coin's Kaufman efficiency ratio over its last 180 completed
  4h closes (the number the live signal already uses to tell a trend from a range), as a
  percentile of that coin's own values at the previous 540 4h closes. Fewer than 180
  earlier values: 0.5.
- **M3 timeframe agreement.** Share of two longer views that agree with the side: the 4h
  SMA 50 against SMA 200, and the last completed daily close against the average of the
  last 200 daily closes. Values 0, 0.5, 1; a view without enough history counts as 0.5.
- **M4 distance from the slow average.** |close - SMA 200 of 4h closes| / close, as a
  percentile of that coin's own values at the previous 540 4h closes. Its sign is open
  (stretched trends may be better or worse), so it is judged in whichever direction it
  shows on A1 only, then held to that direction everywhere else.
- **MC composite.** The plain average of M1, M2 and M3. Fixed now; no weights are fitted.
The hint behind M1: in EXP-2026-023, entries made while other coins were already positioned
the same way earned about twice as much per dollar as the rest, in both A1 and A2. That was
a by-product of a risk-cap test, so it is a hypothesis here, not a finding.

## Step 1: is the score informative?
For every entry of the flat account: the score at the decision, and the trade's result in
R (whole-trade profit or loss after all fees, divided by the planned loss at the opening
stop). Entries are split into thirds by score within the slice (natural levels where the
score has three or fewer values). Reported per slice and measure: entries, mean R and share
of winners per third, and the rank correlation of score with R. Pooled over all slices:
the rank correlation with a 90% bootstrap interval (2,000 draws, entries resampled by
calendar week to respect clustering).
A measure is **informative** if the top third's mean R is above the bottom third's on at
least five of the seven slices, including at least two of the three fresh slices.

## Step 2: does sizing by it beat flat sizing?
Collateral for a new position = low + (high - low) x score. Two ranges around the
account's flat size c: narrow (2/3 c to 4/3 c) and wide (1/2 c to 3/2 c); c is 2% for A
and 1% for B. Each sized account is compared with its flat twin: the same account at a
fixed collateral equal to the sized account's average over its entries on that slice, so
that only the placement of size differs. Reported: return a year, Sharpe, max drawdown,
worst day, return over drawdown, at 1x and 2x costs, and the Sharpe difference with a 90%
paired block-bootstrap interval (10-day blocks, 2,000 draws).
A measure **earns the range** if it is informative and, on the wide range, its Sharpe is
above its twin's on at least five of the seven slices including two of the three fresh
ones, with the narrow range agreeing in sign on at least five.

## What follows from the result
- One or more measures earn the range: the best by pooled rank correlation (the composite
  if it passes) is recommended to the owner, with the range values the owner has chosen.
- None does: that is reported plainly. The range is still built as a setting; each account
  then trades at the fixed point inside it that the owner picks, until a measure passes.
- Nothing is re-tuned after the results are seen. Thresholds, windows and the composite's
  make-up stay as written; anything else is labelled exploratory.

## Harness
`research/exp026_lib.py` on top of the EXP-2026-024 loop: the scores are computed at each
decision from candles that have closed, and the size is set per open through the real
paper account. With sizing off it must reproduce `exp024_lib.run` event for event on A1
and B1. A tamper test changes candles after a decision and shows no score moves.
